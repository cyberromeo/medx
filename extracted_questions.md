A 30-year-old female presents with a symmetrical rash involving her cheeks, sparing the nasolabial folds, after sun exposure. There is no systemic involvement. Which of the following primary lesion types is most likely observed in this patient’s condition?

### Options
- [ ] A. Papule
- [ ] B. Macule
- **[x]** C. Plaque
- [ ] D. Vesicle

**Correct Answer: C**

### Explanation
Correct Answer: C. Plaque A plaque is a raised, flat-topped lesion larger than 1 cm in diameter, often resulting from coalesced papules. ## Question 18

Which of the following is the most likely cause of an increase in end-tidal carbon dioxide tension during general anesthesia with a constant minute ventilation?

### Options
- [ ] A. Embolism
- **[x]** B. Hyperthermia
- [ ] C. Decrease in cardiac output
- [ ] D. Deeper plane of anesthesia

**Correct Answer: B**

### Explanation
Correct Answer ✅ B) Hyperthermia End-tidal CO₂ (ETCO₂) reflects the amount of carbon dioxide exhaled at the end of expiration, which depends on three major factors: CO₂ production (metabolism) Pulmonary perfusion (cardiac output) Alveolar ventilation In this question, minute ventilation is constant, so the rise in ETCO₂ must be due to increased CO₂ production. Hyperthermia and CO₂ Production Conditions such as hyperthermia or malignant hyperthermia cause: Increased metabolic rate Excessive CO₂ production Rapid rise in ETCO₂ This is often one of the earliest signs of malignant hyperthermia during anesthesia. Other associated signs may include: Tachycardia Hyperthermia Muscle rigidity Metabolic acidosis Therefore, hyperthermia leads to increased CO₂ generation and elevated ETCO₂. Why the Other Options are Incorrect A) Embolism Pulmonary embolism causes reduced pulmonary perfusion, which results in decreased ETCO₂, not increased. C) Decrease in cardiac output Lower cardiac output reduces CO₂ transport to the lungs, leading to decreased ETCO₂. D) Deeper plane of anesthesia A deeper anesthetic plane usually depresses metabolism, which may reduce CO₂ production, not increase it. Key Exam Pearls ETCO₂ increases when: Hypermetabolism (e.g., malignant hyperthermia) Increased CO₂ production Hypoventilation ETCO₂ decreases when: Pulmonary embolism Decreased cardiac output Hyperventilation References Miller’s Anesthesia, 9th Edition – Capnography and CO₂ monitoring. Morgan & Mikhail’s Clinical Anesthesiology, 6th Edition – End-tidal CO₂ monitoring. Oxford Handbook of Anaesthesia, 4th Edition – Capnography interpretation.

A 43-year-old patient underwent an emergency minor arm repair procedure after a car accident. A standard dose of lidocaine was administered near the brachial plexus for peripheral nerve block. - [ ] A. Midazolam

**Correct Answer: C**

### Explanation
Correct Answer ✅ C) 20% Intralipid emulsion he patient is most likely experiencing Local Anesthetic Systemic Toxicity (LAST), a serious complication that occurs when local anesthetic drugs (e.g., lidocaine, bupivacaine) enter the systemic circulation in excessive amounts or are injected intravascularly. Mechanism (“Lipid Sink Theory”): Lipid emulsion absorbs lipophilic local anesthetic molecules from plasma and tissues. This reduces the drug concentration at vital organs such as the heart and brain, reversing toxicity. It also improves myocardial energy supply and cardiac function. Therefore, lipid emulsion therapy is the definitive treatment for severe LAST. ## Question 26

A 48yrs old ASA grade 2 patient was being operated for laparoscopic cholecystectomy. All of the following monitor should be attached during intraoperative period except :

### Options
- [ ] A. Capnography
- [ ] B. Neuromuscular monitoring
- [ ] C. Bis monitor
- [ ] D. Invasive blood pressure monitoring

**Correct Answer: N/A**

### Explanation
Correct Answer: Invasive blood pressure monitoring Intraoperative monitoring is essential for patient safety during anesthesia. The American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) recommends standard monitoring for all patients undergoing general anesthesia. These standard monitors include: Electrocardiography (ECG) Non-invasive blood pressure (NIBP) Pulse oximetry Capnography Temperature monitoring Additional monitors may be used depending on type of surgery, patient condition, and anesthetic technique. Capnography Capnography measures end-tidal CO₂ (ETCO₂) and confirms: Adequate ventilation Endotracheal tube placement Early detection of complications such as malignant hyperthermia or hypoventilation It is mandatory during general anesthesia, especially in laparoscopic surgeries, where CO₂ insufflation may increase CO₂ levels. ✔ Therefore, capnography is required. Neuromuscular Monitoring During laparoscopic surgery, neuromuscular blocking agents are often used to: Facilitate endotracheal intubation Provide muscle relaxation Neuromuscular monitoring (e.g., train-of-four monitoring) helps assess: Adequate muscle relaxation Recovery from neuromuscular blockade ✔ Hence it is useful during surgery. BIS Monitoring Bispectral Index (BIS) monitoring assesses the depth of anesthesia by analyzing EEG signals. Although it is not mandatory, it is commonly used in many operating rooms to: Reduce awareness during anesthesia Optimize anesthetic dosing ✔ It may be used but is not essential. Recommended Intraoperative Fluid in Neonates Current pediatric anesthesia recommendations suggest: ➡ Balanced salt solution with 1–2.5% dextrose This provides: Electrolyte balance (from balanced crystalloid) Glucose supply to prevent hypoglycemia Thus, balanced salt solution with 2.5% dextrose is considered appropriate. Why the Other Options are Incorrect B) Isolyte-P Contains electrolytes but may not provide optimal glucose concentration for neonates during surgery. C) Half normal saline (0.45% NaCl) Hypotonic solution, increases risk of hyponatremia and cerebral edema. D) Balanced salt solution + 5% dextrose Excess glucose may cause hyperglycemia, osmotic diuresis, and metabolic disturbances. Key Exam Pearls Neonatal Fluid Considerations Issue Neonatal Risk Hypoglycemia Due to low glycogen stores Hyponatremia With hypotonic fluids Hyperglycemia With excessive dextrose Therefore balanced crystalloids with low-dose dextrose (1–2.5%) are preferred.
